# Ben-Assayag & El-Yaniv (2021) — Train on Small, Play the Large (ScalableAlphaZero)

```yaml

venue_year:      arXiv preprint 2021 (arXiv:2107.08387 [cs.LG], v1 submitted 18 Jul 2021; no journal/conference version found as of 2026-09-26)
question:        Can modelling the board as a graph and swapping AlphaZero's CNN for a GNN let one model train cheaply on small boards and then play — and win on — much larger boards of the same game, without domain-specific input engineering?
game_task:       Othello (primary), Gomoku, Go; training on small boards (5x5–8x8 Othello, 5x5–9x9 Gomoku, up to 9x9 Go), play/evaluation up to 20x20

Network: 3 GIN (Graph Isomorphism Network) layers with layer norm, ReLU and skip connections, two heads; policy head = one fully-connected layer + log-softmax over NODES (node-based actions). Built on PyTorch Geometric. Variable board size is free because message passing fixes only per-node feature dimensions, not input size; symmetry augmentation is dropped on the assumption GNNs are inherently invariant.

compute_budget:  One TITAN X Pascal GPU + one Intel Core i7 per experiment. ScalableAlphaZero: ~3 days on small Othello boards, ~2.5 days Gomoku, ~3 days Go; baseline AlphaZero trained directly on the large target board for up to 30 days. (Full 19x19 Go AlphaZero baseline was unaffordable.)
metrics:         Average outcome over 100 games (win=1, draw=0.5, loss=0), colours alternated, vs three opponents — random, greedy hand-coded heuristic, and AlphaZero trained on the target board size. FIVE independent runs with standard errors (e.g. 0.85±0.02). Headlines: beats the 30-day AlphaZero with ~10x less training — 54% on 16x16 Othello, 84% on 20x20 Othello, 100% on 17x17 Gomoku.

difference_from_our_study:  We keep the general GNN-inside-AlphaZero recipe and, notably, the multi-run reporting discipline (their 5 runs ± SE is the statistical bar Rigaux & Kashima lack; we use 3 seeds/arm with per-seed reporting). Their board graph (fixed lattice, orthogonal-neighbour edges, dummy node) has no direct Hive analogue since Hive has no fixed board; our naive-adjacency ablation is the closest counterpart, and it diverged at parity settings — evidence that a bare adjacency graph is not enough, echoing that their lattice graph still encodes board geometry.
```

## Notes
- The earliest of the GNN-AlphaZero line we cite: replaces the CNN with 3 GIN layers over the square-lattice graph and gets board-size generalisation for free from message passing. The "train on small, play the large" economics (3 GPU-days beating a 30-GPU-day large-board AlphaZero) is the paper's actual claim — it is a transfer/scaling result, NOT evidence that graph beats grid at equal budget on a fixed board, and we should cite it as such in the H8 discussion.
- Statistical practice worth crediting: five independent runs per evaluation with standard errors, single-GPU budget honestly stated, three opponent tiers (random / greedy heuristic / trained AZ) — structurally close to our frozen-opponent ladder. Between the three works read today, this hobby-scale paper has the best replication hygiene; the NeurIPS chess paper has none (1 run), and hiveGo has no evaluation at all.
- Their own stated boundary is exactly where our study lives: node-based actions only ("actions refer to the nodes on the graph"), with chess-like edge actions flagged as future work — the gap Rigaux & Kashima (2024) later filled with edge-featured GAT. Hive is a third case: actions are (piece, destination) pairs on an unbounded hex arrangement, which neither node-lattice nor chess-move-edge formulations cover directly; our shared action decoder across both encoders is our answer to that.
- The dummy-node trick (global node wired to every square) is their fix for long-range information flow through a shallow 3-layer GNN — a cheap alternative to depth. Relevant to interpreting our result: our relational message-passing GNN also has finite receptive depth, and Hive's ring-blocking/freedom-to-move interactions are long-range; worth one line in the H8 discussion of why graph underperformed at small budget.
- No code or weights ever surfaced (promised "upon acceptance"; the paper appears unpublished beyond arXiv), so nothing is reproducible from artifacts — classification stays bibliography (it is a citable preprint), but reproduction claims should lean on the paper's numbers only.
