## Notes

- **This is a direct grid-vs-graph comparison in a hex-grid board game at matched capacity** — the exact shape of RQ-H1. It weakens a "first ever" novelty claim and should be cited as the closest prior work; our differentiators are Hive (stacking, frameless, movement actions), AlphaZero-style training for both arms, and equal *training budget* rather than only equal parameters.
- Architecture detail worth copying: their GNN is a 15-layer SAGEConv stack (~487K params) with dueling heads — advantage computed per node via an extra SAGEConv layer, value via readout. Per-node action scoring works because in Hex every action is "claim this empty cell", i.e., actions biject to nodes. In Hive, actions are (piece, destination) pairs — hence our shared action decoder scoring node-pair candidates instead; edges or pair-embeddings replace their per-node head.
- Their graph formulation is not a plain board graph: it is the Shannon vertex-switching game, where played moves remove/contract nodes, so the graph shrinks as the game progresses and colored stones never appear as nodes. Elegant but Hex-specific — they say so themselves for Go/Chess. Our Hive graph (occupied cells + empty candidate destinations as nodes, hex-adjacency edges with direction attributes, stack info as node features) is the generic alternative; worth an explicit sentence in the paper.
- Empty candidate destinations: in their formulation *all* nodes are empty cells (occupied ones are contracted away). That is one answer to "how to represent empty destinations in a graph": make them first-class nodes. Supports our choice to include candidate-destination nodes rather than only occupied cells.
- Key empirical asymmetry to test for in Hive: GNN wins on long-range dependency puzzles and board-size transfer and overfits less, but the CNN (Gao ResNet) is better at local patterns / raw strength on the trained size. Hive is dominated by local adjacency tactics around the queen — so the CNN arm may be stronger than hex-game intuition suggests; RQ-H1 is genuinely open.
- Budget numbers reusable as reference points: 110 GPU-hours per model (A100) for the DQN comparison; the AlphaZero-style run (GraphAra) needed 3 A100s for ~6 days — an order of magnitude more, relevant to our "limited compute" framing (we sit far below GraphAra).
- Preprint status: cite as arXiv preprint; do not label it NeurIPS/ICLR.
