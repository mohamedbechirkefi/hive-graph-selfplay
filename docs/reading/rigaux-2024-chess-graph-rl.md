## Notes
- The strongest published graph-beats-grid result in board-game RL, and at NeurIPS — this is the paper our negative Hive result must be discussed against. Headline: AlphaGateau "increases playing strength an order of magnitude faster" than a same-framework CNN AlphaZero, and a 10-block model trained on 5x5 Gardner chess transfers: Elo 807±46 on 8x8 with zero 8x8 exposure, 1181±50 after 2–3 fine-tuning iterations, 1876±47 after 100 (comparable to the dedicated 5-block 8x8 model).
- Statistical methodology, verified from the paper: NO seed replication — "each model trained only once". The ±intervals are delta-method CIs on the Elo estimate (Jeffreys prior on match outcomes), i.e. match-sampling uncertainty, not run-to-run variance. Our 3-seeds/arm protocol is strictly stronger on exactly the axis where their evidence is thinnest; in H8's discussion this matters because our per-seed spread showed how easily a single run can mislead at small budget.
- Two confounds to name precisely when contrasting with H1: (1) representation and action parameterisation change together (edge-policy readout vs fixed grid head) — our shared decoder was designed to cut this confound, and it is a genuine design difference, not an oversight on our part; (2) "similar number of parameters" spans 1.0M vs 2.2M — our 1.44M/1.47M matching is much tighter.
- Budget contrast: 8x A5000 for ~2 weeks vs our small-budget regime. Their speed advantage is measured per iteration and per wall-clock at that scale; nothing in the paper establishes the graph advantage survives at budgets like ours (and our pre-registered result says that for Hive it does not).
- Chess gives edges rich, hand-designed semantics (direction, promotion, piece capabilities); Hive's natural graph is occupancy adjacency — far less informative per edge. Together with our naive-adjacency ablation diverging at parity settings, this supports the interpretation that WHAT the edges encode, not graph-ness per se, carries their result.
