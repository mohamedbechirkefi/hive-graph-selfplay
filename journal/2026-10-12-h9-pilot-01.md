## Methods

One run per arm, sequential, under `nohup caffeinate`. Each run: two
generations of 200 self-play games (128/32 simulations, the Study 1
settings), training 2 epochs per generation, generation 1 initialised
from generation 0's final checkpoint (weights only) and trained on the
shards of generations 0 and 1 (window 3 truncated to the two existing
generations), export, then a final evaluation of 20 paired games per
frozen opponent (sanity check of the path only; scores enter no
analysis). Seed 10 and prefix `pilot2` keep the pilot outside Study 1
and outside the Study 2 campaign seeds. No other heavy process ran
beside the pilot. Phase durations below come from file modification
times (self-play manifest, final checkpoint, exported network); the
driver's `wallclock.json` gives the per-generation total.

### Cost projection for the campaign (feeds protocol v2 §4)

Study 1's training time per generation, from the same timestamps on
the runs of seeds 1 to 3: grid 194 to 250 s (0.57 to 0.64 h per run,
3% of the run), graph 516 to 833 s (1.55 to 2.20 h per run, 5%). With
W = 3 the training set is 1, 2, 3, 3, … generations, so training time
over ten generations is about 2.7 times Study 1's: about +1.0 h per
grid run and +3.0 h per graph run. Projected per-run totals: grid 18 to
20 h, graph 30 to 53 h (Study 1's spread plus the window); ten runs
about 295 h, 12 to 13 days sequential, plus about one day of
evaluations. Warm starts may lengthen self-play games earlier in the
run (generation 1's self-play took 18% longer than generation 0's on
the grid arm, 0.4% less on the graph arm); the projection carries that
uncertainty.

## Failures

One pin failed as designed before passing. The stamp check of
`hivenet.checks` required every record of a training set to carry the
stamp of one manifest (Study 1 trained on one generation at a time);
on the windowed set it reported records stamped for generations 0 and
1 against generation 1's manifest alone. The check was extended to
take the manifests of all generations in the window (comma-separated)
and to require the set of record stamps to equal the set of listed
stamps; on a Study 1 shard set (cmp-grid-s1, generation 3) it still
passes with one manifest, and a deliberately wrong manifest still
fails. The driver, the trainers and the data are unaffected. Recorded
in the methodology log (2026-10-12).

Also noted, not a failure: the first invocation of the battery used
relative shard paths, which the data battery (run from `python/`)
cannot resolve; absolute paths are required, as in Study 1.

## Limits / confounders

One seed, two generations, 200 games: the clocks scale the budget
section of protocol v2 but do not predict ten-generation totals exactly
(game length and training-set size both grow with the generation index;
the window reaches its full width W=3 only from generation 2). The
pilot's evaluation volume (20 games) carries no statistical meaning.

## Interpretation

The Study 2 regime runs end to end on both arms: each generation after
the first initialises from the previous checkpoint and trains on the
windowed shards, the records carry the stamps of their generations,
save/resume is bitwise-identical on the windowed set, and training and
evaluation inputs stay separated. The replay window's cost is a
training-time growth proportional to the window's record count on both
arms (factor 2.0 to 2.15 when the second generation enters), which is a
few percent of a run because self-play dominates; the campaign's budget
is Study 1's plus about one hour per grid run and three hours per graph
run. Nothing in the pilot bears on H1′.
