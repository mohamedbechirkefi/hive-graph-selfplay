## Limits / confounders

Five independent seeds per arm; the intervals are wider than Study 1's
on four of six contrasts (B-MCTS same-examples spans 0.17). The two
studies use different seed sets and two regime changes at once (warm
start and window), so the between-study movement is descriptive and
not attributable to either change alone. The hyperparameters are Study
1's untuned defaults in both arms (option A). The grid arm's score
against B-HEU is lower than in Study 1 (0.077 against 0.129), so the
warm-started regime did not raise absolute strength against the strong
opponents within ten generations at these settings; what it changed is
the gap between the arms. The same-wall-clock reading charges the
graph arm a 2.34× cost measured on this machine's providers.

## Interpretation

Applying the frozen rule. (a) Under both readings the graph arm shows
no seed-consistent advantage against any opponent. (b) Every graph−grid
interval includes zero and none shows a graph advantage; the largest
upper bounds are +0.090 (same-examples, against the search opponent,
where both arms score 0.13) and +0.055 (same-wall-clock). The protocol
leaves "meaningful" without a numeric threshold and requires the
largest upper bound to be reported: the rule rejects H1′ for any
threshold above +0.090; at thresholds between +0.055 and +0.090 the
same-wall-clock reading rejects and the same-examples reading leaves a
graph advantage against B-MCTS of up to 0.09 unexcluded; Study 1's
realised bound was +0.028. **H1′ is not supported: no graph advantage
was detected under either reading, and the data cannot exclude a small
one (below about 0.09) against the search opponent.** The verdict is
stated in exactly these terms.

Secondary question: the contrast moved toward zero on every opponent
under both readings relative to Study 1, and changed sign in two of six
cells (B-MCTS same-examples +0.003; B-HEU same-wall-clock +0.013), all
within intervals that include zero. The graph arm's Study 1 deficit
against the random and heuristic opponents is no longer visible at
equal examples and is reduced at equal wall-clock (−0.052 against
B-RND, interval touching zero). The conversion pathology persists in
the graph arm in three seeds of five (24–58% truncation against B-RND)
and is absent in two; under the 0.5 treatment the graph arm still
trails against B-RND under both readings. The ordering of Study 1
therefore does not survive the warm-started regime as a deficit, but
nothing in Study 2 supports the original hypothesis of a graph
advantage.
